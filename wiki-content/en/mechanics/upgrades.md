## Principle

Every tool, weapon or piece of armour has **upgrade slots**, each of a **family**: craft for now (one slot for iron, two for gold and diamond, one for leather and cloth), and the miner's hammer's **head**. Machine modules, runes and staff parts will come as other families. Upgrades are put at the **anvil**, paid in materials and experience (free until experience exists).

## Craft upgrades

- **Sharpening** (tools and weapons): more damage and mining speed.
- **Reinforcement** (tools, weapons, armour): more uses, and more protection for armour.
- **Lightening** (armour): armour that weighs less (speed, then stamina and mana).

Each upgrade has **levels**: the next level replaces the one before, it does not add to it. Reinforcement and lightening do not go together.

## No stacking

As with potions, **the same effect does not add up**: if two upgrades give damage, only the strongest counts.

## The miner's hammer

It digs what a pickaxe digs, slower, but its head widens at the anvil, level after level and dearer each time: 3×1, 5×1, 3×3, 3×5 then 5×5. The area lies across the face aimed at (width × height on a wall; width × depth ahead on a floor). Only the blocks the hammer can mine go with the block aimed at; the break lasts as long as the hardest block of the area; each block wears the hammer; the loot falls in one heap. The "Hammer area" key (R, right stick click) picks a smaller area already unlocked, shown above the hotbar; the area's outline is drawn around the aimed block.

## Repairing

At the anvil, with no upgrade chosen: the item's material gives back a quarter of its uses per unit; a second like item adds its own and 12 % more. Each repair costs a little more experience than the one before.
