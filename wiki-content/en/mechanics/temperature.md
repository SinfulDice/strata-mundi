## How it works

In survival, you feel **hot or cold**. The felt temperature, in degrees, shows above the hotbar on the left: a **scale** from blue (freezing) to red (scorching), pleasant in the middle, with a white marker showing where you stand. Away from comfort, a line gives the zone and the degrees ("Cold 3 °C"); when it gets worse, a tip shows for a few seconds.

The felt temperature slowly follows your surroundings (it takes a good minute to go from one extreme to the other). It adds up:

- the **biome** (the desert burns by day, the tundra freezes);
- the **night**, cooler everywhere, and icy in the desert;
- **altitude**: the higher, the colder;
- **water**: swimming cools you, and you stay **wet** for a while after (you dry three times faster near a fire);
- the **heat** of nearby blocks: fire, lava, torches and lanterns (up close), lit furnaces, the **campfire**; only the strongest counts, and a wall between it and you holds three quarters of it back;
- a **shelter**: a roof over your head keeps half the warmth at night and gives shade by day;
- **clothing**: the wool cloak and the cloth and leather sets keep the cold out (each point brings you 3 degrees back toward comfort, never past it). Beware, wool keeps you warm in the desert too!

## Effects

- **Cold**: you move slower and get hungry faster.
- **Hot**: you get thirsty faster and stamina comes back slower.
- **Freezing** and **scorching**: on top of that, you slowly lose health (freezing, heatstroke). Freezing resistance and fire resistance (equipment, later potions and spells) lessen this damage.

The edges of the screen frost over or glow: the effect can be turned off in the options (Interface). Temperature can be turned off when creating the world; in creative, you feel nothing.

## Tips

A campfire and a wool cloak are enough for a night in the snow. In the desert, travel at dawn, in the shade or with your feet in water, and keep a waterskin.
