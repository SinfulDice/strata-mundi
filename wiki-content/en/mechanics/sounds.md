## How it works

Every sound in the game is **made by the game itself** from recipes: filtered noise for scuffs and gusts, grains for gravel, snow or leaves, resonances for wood, stone, glass and metal, bubbles for water and lava. Each sound comes in several **variants**, and each play shifts its pitch a little: two steps in a row never sound the same.

## What you hear

- **Footsteps**: their sound depends on the block underfoot (stone, dirt, grass, sand, gravel, snow, wood, glass and ice, leaves, metal) and their pace on your speed: you can hear running.
- **Broken and placed blocks**: each material has its own; glass shatters.
- **Water**: a splash when you fall in, strokes as you swim.
- **Lava**: it bubbles now and then.
- **Fire**: it crackles.

Sounds come **from where they happen**: quieter far away, left or right depending on where you look, a little duller from behind.

## Settings

**Sound** tab of the options: master volume, then **blocks**, **ambience** and **music** volumes. Graphics presets never change them. Without a sound card the game runs normally, silently.

## Subtitles

Turned on in the **Sound** tab, they write every sound heard at the bottom right of the screen, with an arrow towards it (← left, → right, ↑ ahead, ↓ behind). A line goes three seconds after the sound was last heard; your own footsteps are not subtitled.

## Resource packs

A resource pack can replace a sound with a recording: a `sounds/<name>.ogg` file takes the place of the recipe with the same name.
