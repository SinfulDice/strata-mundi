## Description

A long ribbon of calm water winding between the hills, lined with pale sand. The river crosses the other biomes without asking their permission, and that's a good thing: it ties the landscapes together like a blue thread.

## Obtaining

Rivers form wherever the ground is low enough, whatever the climate around them: they follow long, winding lines across the land. The river biome only covers the bed itself, under the water; the banks belong to the neighbouring biomes. As the terrain rises, the river fades away and leaves only a dry valley carved into the slope.

## Usage

A natural road for exploring without getting lost, a water source always close at hand, a good supply of sand, and a ready-made moat to protect a house.

## Behaviour

- **Water**: static, up to sea level, like the ocean. It doesn't flow (no current to swim against yet).
- **Bed**: sand, across the whole width.
- **Banks**: gently sloping, with no cliffs or overhangs: you can always walk out.
- **Vegetation**: none in the bed.
- **Altitude**: the higher the terrain, the thinner the river, until only a dry valley remains.
- Even in cold regions, river water doesn't freeze.

## Tips

Lost? Follow a river: it winds through the lowlands, always at sea level, and crosses plenty of landscapes along the way. Where it fades into a dry valley, it makes a handy path to climb into the hills without scrambling.

## Discovery in game

The game doesn't show biome names yet: you recognise a river by its narrow, winding strip of water over a sandy bed. F3 gives your altitude: at the water's edge, you're at sea level.
